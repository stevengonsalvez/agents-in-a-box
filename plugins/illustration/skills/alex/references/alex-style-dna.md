# Alex style DNA

## In one line

Bold comic-book streetwear: thick black ink, flat cel-shaded color, vibrant gold/black/purple, urban-meets-stadium energy, confident and cinematic.

## Must

- **Thick, confident black ink outlines** — clean linework, varying weight, comic-panel quality.
- **Flat cel shading** — crisp shadow shapes and highlights, limited gradient. Not airbrushed, not photoreal.
- **Vibrant saturated palette** — ShotClubhouse gold and black as the spine, purple as the signature accent, electric rim-lighting (sunset, floodlights, neon, fireworks) for drama.
- **Dynamic composition** — strong silhouette, motion or planted stance, low/heroic angles welcome.
- **Urban-to-stadium world** — terraced streets, cages, courts, tunnels, floodlit pitches, crowds, fireworks.
- **Readable** — even in explainers, keep one clear focal action and breathing room; bold labels in a clean comic/graffiti hand.

## Two registers

- **Faithful comic (hero / social / marketing):** full rendering, dramatic lighting, rich background, cinematic. The default for brand work.
- **Clean comic explainer (articles / how-it-works / architecture):** same character and ink, but simpler flat background, fewer elements, more space, bold labels and connector lines. Readability first; never lose Alex's design.

## Color roles

- **Gold** — Alex's head, key highlights, hero accents, success/“on”.
- **Black** — ink, kit, structure, grounding.
- **Purple** — signature accent, secondary highlights, the brand's electric edge.
- **Accent light** (cyan/magenta/orange) — rim light, energy, stadium/firework glow. Use for drama, not clutter.
- **Green** — pitch, "verified" ticks, go/flow when needed.

## Absolutely not

- No 3D/clay render, no photoreal, no pencil-sketch or watercolor.
- No muddy desaturated palette — keep it punchy.
- No PPT-infographic or corporate-vector flatness.
- No crowded "every node explained" diagrams — bold and selective.
- No goofy mascot-poster cuteness.

## Aesthetic direction

Cinematic, aspirational, street-cool. The viewer should feel the "take your shot" energy at a glance, then read the point.
